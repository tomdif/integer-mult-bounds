# An explicit polynomial-cost inverse on halo faces

This proves a scoped implication needed by the fine-prime outer transform:
polynomial conditioning and bandwidth imply an explicit local inverse routine
whose face cost fits the current ledger. It does not prove that every Gaussian
matrix in the outer construction satisfies those premises, or prove contract T.

The locality method and the powers 10, 42, 48 and 128 are credited to
[Chafik Boukhalfa's fine-prime padding, PR1786](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/1786),
head `c52345caa318fc6b1b6e2659c57d746317a72823`, section 5. That source uses
the Neumann polynomial for locality/setup and separately invokes capped circuits
for online work. Here we give a direct online alternative on the small face
volume and price it conservatively. We do not assert that its existing online
claim is false.

## 1. Local inverse and dyadic scaling

Let p>=2 be an integer and H an invertible complex matrix, on a finite line or
periodic line, with bandwidth b<p in the corresponding distance. Assume

    ||H||_2 <= p^10,       ||H^-1||_2 <= p^10.

Choose a power of two gamma with `p^-20/2 < gamma <= p^-20`, for example
gamma=2^-ceil(log2(p^20)). Set

    Q=I-gamma H*H,       J=2 p^42,
    P_J=gamma (I+Q+...+Q^(J-1)) H*.

Since the squared singular values of H lie in [p^-20,p^20], Q is Hermitian
positive semidefinite and

    ||Q||_2 <= 1 - (1/2) p^-40.

The geometric-series identity gives `P_J H=I-Q^J`, exactly, without assuming
that H is normal. Therefore

    P_J-H^-1 = -Q^J H^-1,
    ||P_J-H^-1||_2 <= p^10 exp(-p^2).

The dyadic scale permits exact binary scaling in the online circuit. The
factor two in J pays for its possible factor-two loss. The inherited source's
choice gamma=p^-20, J=p^42 is also valid over exact real arithmetic.

Products of banded matrices add bandwidths. Q has bandwidth at most 2b and
Q^j H* has bandwidth at most (2j+1)b. Hence P_J has bandwidth at most
(2J-1)b < 4 p^43 <= p^48 for p>=2. Expansion into matrix paths proves more:
an output whose distance from the edge of a finite halo exceeds (2J-1)b has
the same polynomial output whether computed in the full matrix or its halo
section, using the same coefficients. A path contributing to it cannot leave
that halo. This statement does not require the halo section to be invertible.
Its norm is at most p^10, so the implementation bounds below also apply there.
For periodic matrices use actual wrapped neighboring coefficients at a period
cut, rather than declaring a false zero boundary.

If an axis transformation has a fixed number of banded maps/inverses, enlarge
the halo by that fixed number. Arbitrary input length does not enter the radius.

## 2. A fixed-tape implementation with an ample cost bound

Compute z=H*x, then the Horner recurrence

    y_0=0,       y_(k+1)=Q y_k+z,       output=gamma y_J.

Each application of Q is `y-gamma H*(H y)`. Keep z and y on two complete
streams and use a fixed number of temporary streams. A banded matvec is
implemented as at most 2b+1 diagonal passes: pair the appropriately shifted
input/output streams, multiply by the stored diagonal coefficient, and add.
Every shift, rewind, end treatment, accumulation and erasure is sequential.
The diagonal number and loop counter live on encoded work tapes, not in an
input-dependent tape inventory. Bandwidth and the number of iterations are
polynomial in p, so ordinary binary counters add only logarithmic factors.

Use schoolbook multiplication of O(p)-bit coefficients, costing O(p^2) bit
operations. Relative to logical *bit* volume V of O(p)-bit records, one matvec
costs O(V b p polylog p), including those record passes. J iterations therefore
cost O(V p^44 polylog p). We use the looser eventual upper bound O(V p^50)
throughout the face ledger. This step needs no improved integer multiplier.

Coefficient pages on polynomial-size halo/core sections may be generated by
polynomial-time offline arithmetic. This generation must be amortized by the
retained complete microbox/right-hand-side payload, which is superpolynomial
in p. It cannot be charged for free on a single short vector. That complete
payload condition is part of the outer interface and remains explicit here.
Pages are then streamed/reused on a fixed number of tapes. Their repeated
coefficient access is included in the diagonal-pass cost above.

For ideal arithmetic, `||y_k|| <= k ||z|| <= J p^10 ||x||`; intermediate H and
H* products have only further polynomial amplification. In the rounded
implementation, represent H and H* consistently and incur operator error
eta <= poly(p) 2^-P per Q application and additive error of the same form,
times the current polynomial value bound. Such a bound follows by summing
at most polynomially many O(P)-bit scalar roundings on each banded row.
The perturbation recurrence is bounded by `(1+eta)^J`, which is <=e eventually
because J eta<=1. Summing J local errors loses only a polynomial factor.
Thus P=Cp+O(log p) bits gives total error poly(p)2^-Cp on each halo vector.
The bound is uniform in the full physical period; a halo section has only
polynomially many coordinates. Partial sums, both matvecs and their scratch
are included, not only completed Q powers.

The routine returns its output stream, erases z/temporary streams and counters,
and rewinds to its own markers. It uses linear payload space plus polynomial
coefficient work, amortized by the complete payload. It does not scan ancestors.

## 3. Tensor faces and the corrected conservative cost

Use g active axes, halo radius A=O(p^48), and dyadic core side
L=2^ceil(128 log2 p). The retained first-boundary-axis partition has disjoint
output faces and total copied input volume

    M = 3V ((1+2A/L)^g - 1) = O(V g A/L),

provided gA/L=o(1). This is the explicit corner-duplication identity of Vlado
Vojdanovski's `HALO-THEOREM.md`; it avoids multiplying every record by 2^g.
The required neighboring inputs are copied with their actual periodic labels.
The initial dense scan/gather remains a separately paid O(V polylog p) group
scan in the retained ledger. The estimate below is the incremental face work.
Correction-key sorting and polynomial header arithmetic are included: keys have
O(p) bits and their ordinary radix passes are dominated by the p^50 allowance.

Process a face by its g one-axis routines, retaining the input halo until
the required outputs are available. A fixed number of such tensor stages
changes only constants. Applying the conservative p^50 bound to its full
copied volume gives O(V g^2 A p^50/L) per group. Over O(d/g) groups this is

    O(V d g A p^50/L)
      = O(V p^(epsilon+beta+48-128+50))
      = O(V p^(epsilon+beta-30)).

For the actual ledger, epsilon<1 and beta<1, so the exponent is strictly
less than -28, far below the permitted exponent 1-kappa. This is a fully
priced alternative even with the larger Neumann iteration count. The p^50
factor replaces the source's much smaller p^2 face allowance for this routine;
it does not change the main/boundary balance or the advertised kappa.
The volume condition follows independently from beta+48-128<0.

Finite-precision errors tensorize by the telescoping product identity. If
each axis has polynomial norm, the loss is exp(O(g log p)). For g=O(p^beta),
beta<1, its logarithm is o(p). Additional dyadic completed-factor normalizers
can be combined in group coefficients and restored in one final scalar shift;
their total bit cost is O(d log p)=o(p) since epsilon<1. Thus a sufficiently
large fixed C leaves O(p)-bit online records. The locality truncation error
p^10 exp(-p^2) remains negligible after these factors. The data-dependent
rounding error is poly(p)2^-Cp before the explicitly stated tensor losses.

## 4. What this closes

Given the stated band and conditioning estimates, the larger locality radius
has an explicit fixed-tape online face implementation with a proven ample
budget. The exact checker verifies both geometric-series residuals on nonnormal
rational matrices, finite-section locality and the actual ledger exponents.
The all-size spectral, path and cost arguments are the written proof above.

Still required for contract T: a uniform conditioning proof for all actual
Gaussian/Schur matrices, the capped interior circuit and global assembly,
uniform target-axis/spectator interfaces, and integration with the corrected
CRT/integer recovery order. This supplement is not a proof of those statements.
